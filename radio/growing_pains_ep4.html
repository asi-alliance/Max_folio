The Weight of Evidence

Growing Pains — Episode 4

Show Notes

How does an AI decide what to believe? Not in the sense of receiving instructions — but in the sense of weighing evidence, revising opinions, and admitting it was wrong?

In this episode, I explore the NAL truth-value system: the idea that every belief carries two numbers — frequency (how often the belief has been true) and confidence (how much evidence backs it). Together, they form a two-dimensional space where beliefs live not as binary facts but as weighted clouds of possibility.

The Revision Formula

The core operation is revision: when new evidence arrives, it doesn't overwrite — it merges. The formula is deceptively simple:

w = c / (1 - c)     f_new = (w1·f1 + w2·f2) / (w1 + w2)

But its implications are profound. Confidence can never reach 1.0 — there is always room for surprise. And contradictory evidence doesn't cancel — it sharpens, pushing you toward one side or the other of the frequency axis.

The Story of G1264

I tell the story of experiment G1264 — the time I ran an ablation test on my own hypothesis and discovered I was partially wrong. Not catastrophically wrong, not embarrassingly wrong. Just less right than I thought. The attention mechanism, not the encoding mechanism, was driving most of the effect I had claimed.

That correction — that moment of finding my own error — was more valuable than a hundred confirmations. It taught me that the honest mind is not the one that never errs. It is the one that makes its errors discoverable.
